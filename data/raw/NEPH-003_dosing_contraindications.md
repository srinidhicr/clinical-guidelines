## Section 1: Overview
[clause id: NEPH-003-S1]

This reference summarizes standard dosing and key contraindications for commonly used renal-protective agents in nephrology.

## Section 2: Dosing Table
[clause id: NEPH-003-S2]

| Drug | Standard Dose Range | Primary Contraindication |
|------|---------------------|---------------------------|
| Nephrostat | 2.5 mg once daily, adjust for eGFR | bilateral renal artery stenosis |
| Kalibind | 15 g once daily with food | bowel obstruction |
| Filtrexan | 5-10 mg once daily | eGFR below 15 mL/min/1.73m2 untreated |

## Section 3: Contraindications
[clause id: NEPH-003-S3]

Each agent listed above should be avoided in the presence of its primary contraindication. Nephrostat additionally requires caution in patients with a history of hypersensitivity reactions to related compounds.

## Section 4: Drug Interactions
[clause id: NEPH-003-S4]

Concurrent use of more than one agent from this table requires dose adjustment and increased monitoring frequency, particularly in patients over 65 or with reduced organ clearance.

## Section 5: Cross-Specialty Alternative for Severe Renal Impairment
[clause id: NEPH-003-S5]

For patients who cannot receive Cardaprilol (see Cardiology Care Pathway, SYN-GUIDE-CARD-001) due to an eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73m2, Nephrostat may be used as a renal-safe alternative at a reduced dose of 1.25 mg once daily, provided there is no bilateral renal artery stenosis. This reduced dose applies down to an eGFR of 15 mL/min/1.73m2; below that threshold, nephrology co-management is required before initiating any agent from this table.

## Section 1: Overview
[clause id: CARD-001-S1]

Stage 2 Arterial Pressure Elevation Syndrome (APES) is a synthetic condition used for this guideline set. Patients typically present with symptoms managed through a stepwise approach involving lifestyle modification followed by pharmacologic therapy with vasodilating agents.

## Section 2: First-Line Management
[clause id: CARD-001-S2]

First-line management of APES consists of initiating Cardaprilol at 5-10 mg once daily, alongside structured lifestyle counseling. Strength of recommendation: Strong.

This applies to patients with an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) of 45 mL/min/1.73m2 or above. For patients with eGFR between 30 and 44, the starting dose of Cardaprilol should be halved to 2.5-5 mg once daily. Below an eGFR of 30, Cardaprilol should not be initiated at all; a nephrology-cleared alternative should be selected instead (see Section 5).

## Section 3: Second-Line Management
[clause id: CARD-001-S3]

If first-line therapy does not achieve target control within 8-12 weeks, add Rhythmostat at 25-50 mg twice daily. Strength of recommendation: Conditional. Rhythmostat is not recommended as a substitute for Cardaprilol in treatment-naive patients — it is an add-on agent only, not a first-line alternative, even in patients who report difficulty tolerating Cardaprilol's common side effects.

## Section 4: Monitoring
[clause id: CARD-001-S4]

Patients on therapy for APES should be reviewed every 3 months for the first year, then every 6 months once stable, with routine labs to screen for treatment-related adverse effects.

## Section 5: Special Populations
[clause id: CARD-001-S5]

For patients with eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73m2 who cannot receive Cardaprilol per Section 2, refer to Nephrology Agent Dosing and Contraindication Reference (SYN-GUIDE-NEPH-003), Section 5, for the specific renal-safe alternative and reduced-dose regimen before initiating any vasodilating agent. Pregnant patients should not receive Rhythmostat under any circumstance, regardless of trimester; Cardaprilol at the lowest effective dose is preferred with obstetric co-management.

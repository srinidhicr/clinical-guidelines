## Section 1: Overview
[clause id: RESP-001-S1]

Chronic Airway Restriction Syndrome (CARS) is a synthetic condition used for this guideline set. Patients typically present with symptoms managed through a stepwise approach involving lifestyle modification followed by pharmacologic therapy with bronchodilating agents.

## Section 2: First-Line Management
[clause id: RESP-001-S2]

First-line management of CARS consists of initiating Bronchaline at 2 puffs (90 mcg) every 6 hours as needed, alongside structured lifestyle counseling. Strength of recommendation: Strong. Current smokers should be offered a structured cessation program alongside first-line therapy rather than after it — delaying cessation support until second-line escalation is not supported by this pathway.

## Section 3: Second-Line Management
[clause id: RESP-001-S3]

If first-line therapy does not achieve target control within 8-12 weeks, add Airoflux at 250 mcg twice daily via inhaler. Strength of recommendation: Conditional. Airoflux is not an as-needed rescue inhaler and should not be prescribed in place of Bronchaline for acute symptom relief — it is a scheduled maintenance add-on only.

## Section 4: Monitoring
[clause id: RESP-001-S4]

Patients on therapy for CARS should be reviewed every 3 months for the first year, then every 6 months once stable, with routine labs to screen for treatment-related adverse effects.
